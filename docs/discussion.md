## 4.1 Interpretation of Predictive Results (RQ1)

The observed overall ordering on every reported metric, under the present configuration, is **LR < MLP < LSTM < TFT** (lower error first; § 3.8.1 point estimates, § 3.8.2 paired moving-block bootstrap intervals at L = 96). All six pairwise MSE intervals exclude zero. RQ1 — *under identical task and information constraints, do more complex models outperform simpler baselines?* — is therefore answered in the negative under the present configuration: each step up the architectural-complexity ladder is associated with a higher observed error. *Interval estimates are used as comparative uncertainty evidence, not as a claim of strict independent-sample inference.*

Two counterintuitive observations are part of this finding: LR outperforms MLP (mean MSE diff −1.27, 95 % CI [−1.58, −0.88]; SUSPECT-02) and MLP outperforms LSTM (−3.14, 95 % CI [−4.20, −2.09]; SUSPECT-03). Both cleared the Counterintuitive-Result Trigger (5 of 7 items PASS; residual two deferred per the disaggregation doctrine and § 2.10.3). The LR-leads-MLP observation has independent literature support in recent work showing that simple linear baselines can match or outperform transformer-class architectures on long-sequence time-series benchmarks including the ETT family (Zeng et al., 2023). The LSTM-above-MLP ordering is harder to ground in canonical literature — the closest framing is the capacity / configuration trade-off analysis of Ouyang, Ravier and Jabloun (2022) — and admits a partial mechanical reading anchored to methodology § 2.10.6: under the locked hidden width of 64, every forecast signal must pass through a single 64-dimensional hidden state before the 168-dimensional output head, whereas the MLP connects its 672-dimensional input directly to the 168-dimensional output through two 128-dimensional hidden layers with no analogous compression stage. Part of the observed gap is therefore consistent, under the present configuration, with a capacity-for-this-task constraint imposed by the 64-dimensional bottleneck rather than with a model-class inferiority claim. Both framings are preliminary and require further validation under the caveats of § 4.4.

The TFT is the worst of the four core models on overall MSE (TFT-ORDER-01); the ordering is robust under the loss-symmetric training cycle of S-02b and is preserved under the v2 fair-core protocol. The interpretation is bounded on two axes. First, this thesis used the locked default `pytorch-forecasting` hyperparameters for TFT without a hyperparameter search, in line with the comparable-tuning-budget clause of § 2.5. Second, the TFT is also the smallest of the four models by parameter count (TFT 18 261 < LSTM 29 608 < LR 113 064 < MLP 124 328; `results/bachelor_safe_v2/complexity_metrics.csv`); per § 2.10.6, part of the TFT-worst ordering is consistent with a capacity-constraint observation anchored to the `hidden_size = 16` default rather than with a transformer-family-inferiority claim. Under a richer tuning budget or a parameter-budget-matched variant the ordering could differ (§ 4.4.3).

**TFT seed-sensitivity under the five-seed v2 protocol.** Expanding from three to five seeds surfaces a TFT-specific initialisation sensitivity that the three-seed baseline could not expose. Seed 1024 converges to `best_epoch = 1` (val-loss 5.84 → 5.31 at epoch 1, then drift in the 5.3–5.8 range through the patience window), yielding an original-scale MSE of 26.50 against a 12.63–14.99 range for the other four seeds (`results/bachelor_safe_v2/tft_metrics.csv`). The resulting TFT mean ± std of **16.34 ± 5.74** is roughly an eight-fold variance inflation against the three-seed v1 estimate (14.19 ± 0.68). The ordering LR < MLP < LSTM < TFT is preserved even when seed 1024 is included, but the observation is evidence that the three-seed estimate under-sampled the tail of TFT's seed-initialisation distribution. It is carried to § 4.4.1 Internal validity as a reportable artefact of the fair-core v2 protocol, not as a TFT-architecture indictment.

Two framing points close this section. The **ensemble effect** (ENSEMBLE-EFFECT-01): seed-averaged ensemble MSE drops by 0.40 (MLP), 0.75 (LSTM), and 1.39 (TFT) relative to per-seed metric means, proportional to per-seed prediction variance (Jensen's inequality for squared-error loss); the ranking is preserved under both reporting choices. The **three-way orthogonality of complexity** (§ 3.8.3): parameter-count (MLP > LR > LSTM > TFT), architectural-category (LR < MLP < LSTM < TFT), and accuracy (LR best, TFT worst) rank the four models along different axes. RQ1 is most cleanly answered when complexity is read as architectural category — along that axis the present configuration produces a monotone anti-correlation with accuracy; along the parameter-count axis no such monotone relationship holds.

## 4.2 Interpretation of Explainability Results (RQ2)

RQ2 — *how trustworthy are the model explanations when assessed with a common model-agnostic explanation instrument on ETTh1?* — is answered under the v6.1 instrumentation introduced in Methodology § 2.9.1–2.9.4. The primary cross-model instrument is model-agnostic occlusion importance (`src/explainability/common_importance.py`); the primary faithfulness metric is AOPC (`src/explainability/faithfulness_test.py`). Per the interpretability contract of this thesis, interpretability is operationalised at the level of aggregated, model-level feature-importance comparison; faithfulness is **tested**, not assumed. A separate explanation-stability layer was dropped in the S-11 archive migration on 2026-04-20; stability evidence in this thesis is therefore restricted to the seed-CV signal present in the five-seed training pipeline. The explanation results are described as **preliminary but directionally supportive under the present configuration**: the final reproducibility validation for the v2 artefacts of the explainability scripts remains pending, and the interpretation below is bounded by the comparability and interpretation checks of § 2.5.

**Cross-model rank agreement on the common instrument.** Because occlusion is the same measurement object for all four models, the six pairwise Spearman ρ on seed-averaged per-variable occlusion importance (`results/bachelor_safe_v2/xai_agreement_occlusion.csv`) are directly comparable: LR↔MLP 0.000, LR↔LSTM −0.179, LR↔TFT 0.500, **MLP↔LSTM 0.750**, MLP↔TFT 0.357, LSTM↔TFT 0.500. The strongest agreement is between the two gradient-trained neural models (MLP↔LSTM); LR disagrees with both other post-hoc-tested models on the rank-trend. The three TFT pairs land in a 0.36–0.50 range, no longer conflated with a SHAP↔VSN method-family gap — because all four rankings are now produced by the same instrument, any residual cross-model disagreement is attributable to model behaviour rather than to a construct asymmetry in the explanation instrument.

**Per-model AOPC faithfulness.** The per-(model, seed) AOPC values (`results/bachelor_safe_v2/faithfulness_aopc.csv`) seed-average to LSTM 0.231, LR 0.202, TFT 0.180, MLP 0.126. Higher AOPC indicates a stronger behavioural separation between the model's top-ranked and bottom-ranked variables under perturbation — i.e. a more faithful ranking. LSTM attains the highest AOPC, consistent with a bottom-of-ranking consensus across all five LSTM seeds on LUFL (its rankings place LUFL last in 5/5 seeds; see `results/bachelor_safe_v2/faithfulness_aopc_per_k.csv`). LR attains a high AOPC driven primarily by the k = 1 contribution — occluding the single most-important variable produces a catastrophic MSE increase on the linear model, while occluding the least-important variable is near-inert — and the across-k dispersion is accordingly large (gap_std across k ≈ 1.18). MLP exhibits the lowest AOPC: the top-bottom separation under its own ranking is small across all k, which — read with the comparability and interpretation discipline of this thesis — is a descriptive observation about the MLP's ranking behaviour, not a causal claim about MLP interpretability.

**TFT AOPC instability.** The TFT's across-seed AOPC standard deviation is 0.147 — roughly twice LSTM's (0.067) and three times MLP's (0.049). Seed 123 produces an AOPC of −0.013 (i.e. bottom-k occlusion marginally hurts the TFT more than top-k occlusion), and seed 1024 — the same seed that converges to `best_epoch = 1` in § 4.1 — has the highest occlusion baseline MSE (1.057 vs 0.631–0.656 for the other four TFT seeds). The across-seed AOPC variance therefore co-varies with the accuracy-side seed sensitivity documented in § 4.1: TFT's initialisation-sensitivity under the v2 five-seed protocol is visible on both the accuracy axis and the faithfulness axis. This does not change the RQ2 rank-correlation reading, but it is an internal-validity observation carried to § 4.4.1.

Auxiliary architecture-specific explanations remain available at `results/bachelor_safe_v2/`: SHAP attributions for LR / MLP / LSTM (`shap_lr.csv`, `shap_mlp.csv`, `shap_lstm.csv` — the LSTM run is true SHAP via `GradientExplainer` but bounded to 16 background / 16 evaluation windows) and the TFT Variable Selection Network importance vector (`tft_importance.csv`, architecture-native via `interpret_output` rather than a SHAP explainer). They are recorded in § 4.4.2 as the construct-validity motivation for introducing the common instrument, but they do not enter the primary RQ2 reading above.

## 4.3 Trade-off Analysis (RQ3)

RQ3 — *is there a practical trade-off between forecasting accuracy and interpretability, and which model achieves the best balance on ETTh1?* — is answered under the v6.1 instrumentation by a two-axis inspection of the four core models on (overall MSE, AOPC) coordinates. The accuracy axis is the seed-averaged original-scale MSE (`results/bachelor_safe_v2/per_seed_metrics.csv`): LR 7.66 / MLP 9.47 / LSTM 12.92 / TFT 16.34. The interpretability axis is the seed-averaged AOPC (`results/bachelor_safe_v2/faithfulness_aopc.csv`): LSTM 0.231 / LR 0.202 / TFT 0.180 / MLP 0.126. The trade-off scatter with across-seed AOPC error bars is rendered at `results/bachelor_safe_v2/trade_off_plot.png`; a per-model AOPC-by-k decomposition is at `faithfulness_aopc_by_k.png`.

**No single model dominates both axes under the present configuration.** The accuracy ordering (lower-first) is LR < MLP < LSTM < TFT; the AOPC ordering (higher-first) is LSTM > LR > TFT > MLP. Under a strong-form trade-off claim we would expect a monotone inverse relationship between accuracy and faithfulness; the observed ordering is not monotone in either direction. LR attains the lowest MSE and the second-highest AOPC, and in this limited sense occupies the most attractive corner of the plot — but its AOPC is driven primarily by the k = 1 contribution, as § 4.2 records, and its across-k dispersion is the largest of the four models. The MLP is the second-most-accurate model but has the lowest AOPC of the four. The LSTM has the highest AOPC but is the third-most-accurate model. The TFT sits worst on accuracy and third on AOPC, with the largest across-seed AOPC standard deviation (0.147). RQ3 in this thesis is therefore answered as follows: **no strong-form trade-off between predictive accuracy and behavioural-faithfulness is observed under the present configuration**; *which* model achieves the best balance depends on the relative weight assigned to the two axes, and under the adopted instrumentation the linear baseline is the most attractive single-point choice on ETTh1.

The answer is **criterion-bounded**. The AOPC is defined as the mean across k ∈ {1, …, 7} of `(MSE(top-k occluded) − baseline) − (MSE(bot-k occluded) − baseline)`; it inherits the behavioural-fidelity framing of Samek et al. (2017) but the k-range choice materially shapes the per-model reading. LR's advantage is k = 1-heavy: the linear model is exquisitely sensitive to the single most-important variable and almost insensitive to the least-important one, so the k = 1 gap dominates the mean. At k = 7 the top-k and bot-k sets coincide and the gap is zero by construction; this degenerate tail is included so that the maximum AOPC is bounded by a per-model property (baseline MSE) rather than by the k-range choice itself. A richer faithfulness metric — continuous infidelity measures (Yeh et al., 2019), ordered-masking damage curves, or AUC-under-masking-curve diagnostics — would be needed to test RQ3's strong form under a criterion less sensitive to k = 1 dominance; such a metric is flagged as future work in § 4.6.

The cross-model occlusion rank agreement of § 4.2 (MLP↔LSTM ρ = 0.75; LR↔MLP ρ = 0.00; three TFT pairs in 0.36–0.50) qualifies the RQ3 answer further: the four models do not share a common ranking anchor, so the AOPC values are computed on per-(model, seed) rankings that are themselves partially divergent. This is a deliberate property of the metric — each model is tested against *its own* importance ordering — but it means that "the best balance" cannot be read as "the model whose single ranking is most behaviourally meaningful for the whole fleet of four", only as "the model whose own ranking is most behaviourally meaningful for itself, evaluated under a common instrument". Under the comparability and interpretation checks the reading stays at the *observed* and *descriptive* level; a causal interpretation of the trade-off shape is not attempted in this thesis.

## 4.4 Threats to Validity

This section enumerates the threats to validity in the three named subsections required by the validation criteria.

### 4.4.1 Internal validity

Seven internal-validity items are recorded.

*TFT seed-42 best-epoch (SUSPECT-01).* Best epoch 5 is borderline to the {1, 2, 3, 4} early-convergence trigger, but the validation-loss trajectory descends 41 / 51 / 54 % from epoch 0 to best epoch across seeds 42 / 123 / 456 — the trajectory is not flat, no early-stopping pathology evidence, recorded for transparency only.

*TFT seed-variance gain (resolved).* Diagnostic-cycle TFT had relative per-seed MSE std 23.5 % (TFT-SEED-VAR-01); the thesis-valid cycle (post loss-symmetry fix S-02b aligning TFT with the MLP / LSTM MSE objective) has 4.8 %, a fivefold reduction attributed to the fix. Prior diagnostic-cycle numbers are not thesis evidence.

*Ensemble-vs-single distinction.* ENSEMBLE-EFFECT-01 model-dependent reductions are documented in § 4.1; the thesis reports both conventions (per-seed mean in § 3.8.1, ensemble in § 3.8.2) and the ordering holds under both.

*Sliding-window independence.* ETTh1 sliding windows overlap by construction, violating the standard-bootstrap (IID) independence assumption. Under the v6.1 protocol the uncertainty layer is a **paired moving-block bootstrap** at block length L = 96 = `INPUT_LEN` (`arch.bootstrap.MovingBlockBootstrap` in `src/evaluation/post_training_analysis.py`; Politis and White (2004) block-length heuristic), which preserves within-block serial dependence rather than silently assuming it away. The residual dependence risk is thereby method-matched to the data structure; the safety sentence continues to apply wherever intervals are quoted. Block-length sensitivity at L ∈ {24, 48, 96, 192} is reported in Appendix § F.2 and confirms L = 96 is not a knife-edge choice.

*TFT seed 1024 outlier under the v2 five-seed protocol.* Discussed in § 4.1 above; recorded here as an Internal-validity item. Three-seed v1 samples did not include seed 1024; the five-seed v2 protocol surfaced `best_epoch = 1` for that seed (MSE 26.50, ≈ 2× the other four seeds' upper bound), lifting TFT's std from 0.68 to 5.74. The ordering LR < MLP < LSTM < TFT is preserved.

*Checkpoint and seed isolation.* The fail-fast guard at restore-best (§ 3.4) was added as a code-robustness pass; in the locked baseline runs it never fires.

*Clipping-symmetrisation audit (CLIP-SYMMETRY-01).* The diagnostic-cycle training protocols for MLP, LSTM and TFT differed in one inherited regulariser: the `pytorch-forecasting` TFT trainer applies `gradient_clip_val = 0.1` by default, while the MLP and LSTM trainers in `src/training/multi_seed.py` applied no clipping. S-12 (2026-04-21) symmetrised this via a single `clip_grad_norm_(max_norm=0.1)` call in the shared `train_with_early_stopping` helper at `src/training/multi_seed.py:133` (LR is closed-form OLS; deterministic-baseline exception applies). The training-loss-symmetry item was re-audited and recorded PASS on 2026-04-21. Methodology § 2.10.5 documents the full pre / post delta table: seed-mean shifts are ≈ 0 % (LR, MLP, TFT) and +0.3 % (LSTM); the overall ordering is preserved. A symmetry correction rather than a hyperparameter tune.

*Device and precision asymmetry (CPU FP32 vs GPU TF32).* MLP and LSTM train on CPU FP32; TFT on GPU under `torch.set_float32_matmul_precision("high")` (PyTorch Lightning default for Ampere-class and newer GPUs, standard in the ETTh1 transformer-family benchmarks of Zhou et al., 2021; Zeng et al., 2023). Methodology § 2.10.6 records the observed impact as sub-fourth-decimal; carried here as an internal-validity caveat.

### 4.4.2 Construct validity

Four construct-validity items are recorded.

*Interpretability is operationalised narrowly.* Per the interpretability constraints of this thesis, interpretability here means **aggregated, model-level feature-importance comparison**, not a claim of causal understanding or a mechanistic account of why specific features matter. The v6.1 plan introduced a **model-agnostic common explanation instrument** — occlusion importance (§ 2.9.1) — precisely to dissolve the construct asymmetry that the earlier SHAP ↔ VSN pairing carried: SHAP (Lundberg and Lee, 2017) is a post-hoc attribution layer on a fixed prediction function, while VSN (Lim et al., 2021) is an architecture-native component whose weights are co-optimised with the rest of the TFT, and these are not equivalent measurement objects. The v1 auxiliary SHAP↔VSN rank-agreement values retained for reference (SHAP-family pairs converging at Spearman 0.786 / 0.857 / 0.714; SHAP↔VSN pairs involving the TFT at 0.357 / 0.107 / 0.286) documented the asymmetry empirically but could not themselves close it. Under the v2 occlusion instrument, all four models are now measured with the same object; the residual cross-model rank-disagreement visible in the occlusion matrix (LR↔MLP 0.00, MLP↔LSTM 0.75, TFT pairs 0.36–0.50) is therefore attributable to model-behaviour differences rather than to a construct gap in the instrument. Interpretability in this thesis nevertheless remains a narrow construct: it does not claim causal understanding, and AOPC measures *behavioural* faithfulness of the ranking, not the explanation's semantic validity. Richer operationalisations (perturbation-stability, continuous infidelity, human-rated usefulness) are flagged as future work in § 4.6.

*Per-variable visibility constraint.* The § 2.10.1 aggregation contract reports a single scalar mean over the (windows × 24 × 7) tensor; per-variable claims are blocked by the disaggregation doctrine. A per-variable evaluation path is future work (§ 4.6).

*Loss-magnitude asymmetry.* The `nn.MSELoss()` reduction in the MLP / LSTM training path collapses to a single scalar mean over `(batch × horizon × variable)`; the `MultiLoss([MSE() for 7])` construction in the TFT path sums seven per-feature MSE terms (≈ 7× magnitude). Gradient direction is identical; Adam's second-moment normalisation together with the TFT learning rate `1 × 10^{-3}` absorbs the magnitude difference. The "training-loss symmetry" requirement is therefore interpreted as **gradient-direction symmetry, not loss-magnitude symmetry**, per methodology § 2.10.6. Retraining under unified scalar-mean MSE is outside bachelor scope.

*Criterion-dependence of the AOPC faithfulness metric.* The RQ3 trade-off analysis of § 4.3 uses AOPC across k ∈ {1, …, 7} as the interpretability axis. The k-range choice materially shapes the per-model AOPC: the LR's AOPC is driven primarily by the k = 1 contribution — occluding the single most-important variable produces a catastrophic MSE increase on the linear model, while occluding the least-important one is near-inert — and the across-k dispersion is accordingly large (gap_std ≈ 1.18; `results/bachelor_safe_v2/faithfulness_aopc_by_k.png`). At k = 7 the top-k and bot-k sets coincide and the gap collapses to zero by construction; this degenerate tail is deliberately included in the mean so that the AOPC is bounded above by a per-model baseline-MSE property rather than by the k-range choice. The "LR is the most attractive single-point choice" observation of § 4.3 is therefore a **criterion-bound** claim: it holds under AOPC on occlusion ranks with k ∈ {1, …, 7}, would need to be re-examined under a continuous infidelity metric (Yeh et al., 2019), an ordered-masking damage curve, or an AUC-under-masking-curve diagnostic, and does not support a stronger-form claim about LR-superior explanation quality in general. Richer faithfulness metrics are flagged as future work in § 4.6.

### 4.4.3 External validity

Four external-validity items are recorded.

*Single dataset.* All results are on ETTh1 (Zhou et al., 2021); no general-domain generalisation is claimed. A multi-dataset extension is future work.

*Locked default tuning budget for TFT.* The TFT was run with the locked default `pytorch-forecasting` hyperparameters (hidden size 16, 4 heads, dropout 0.1, lr 1e-3) without a hyperparameter search, consistent with the comparable-tuning-budget clause of § 11 (no model received an asymmetric budget). The TFT-worst observation of § 4.1 is therefore bounded by the budget under which it was obtained; a richer search could change the ordering.

*Equalised past-only information regime.* `time_varying_known_reals=[]` is required by § 2.5 so all four models receive identical information at prediction time. This removes the TFT's native support for known-future covariates (e.g. calendar variables), which the other three architectures cannot consume anyway. The TFT-worst observation of § 4.1 should be read as *given the same information as a linear baseline on this dataset, the TFT does not produce a lower error*; a re-deployment with known-future calendar covariates would lift this constraint and could yield a different ordering. That alternative thesis is methodologically distinct and not pursued here.

*Non-canonical chronological split (60 / 20 / 20 row-based).* This thesis partitions ETTh1 at 60 / 20 / 20 on a row basis (methodology § 2.3); parts of the ETTh1 transformer-family literature use a 12 / 4 / 4-month calendar-based partition (Zhou et al., 2021; Zeng et al., 2023). Effective forecast span + split-then-window protocol recorded in methodology § 2.3.1 (METHOD-NOTE-01); § 2.10.6 carries the divergence as an acknowledged design asymmetry. External benchmark comparisons in this chapter must point back to METHOD-NOTE-01 rather than treat numbers as apples-to-apples. Recorded here as an external-validity caveat rather than a fairness-protocol violation, since all four core models share identical partition boundaries.

## 4.5 Generalisability

The framework itself is the reusable contribution. The dataset-agnostic components — split-then-window preprocessing, best-checkpoint training, frozen-tensor prediction export, four-axis original-scale evaluation, paired-bootstrap uncertainty, and the parameter-count / arch / wall-clock complexity layer — would carry over to any hourly multivariate forecasting task with a similar horizon structure. The Fairness and Comparability Protocol (§ 2.5) and the associated Comparability Gate are independent of the specific models and would constrain any fair-core extension to other architectures. Task-specific parts are the four model implementations and the SHAP / VSN explainability branch, both of which carry pytorch-forecasting and SHAP-library-specific assumptions.

## 4.6 Future Work

Five concrete future-work items are identified.

*Per-variable disaggregation export.* A per-horizon path is already in place (`src/evaluation/per_horizon_metrics.py`, VALIDATED 2026-04-21) and the comparability and interpretation checks admit descriptive per-horizon observations — the non-monotonic LSTM h = 24 < h = 12 and TFT h = 24 < h = 12 pattern consistent with ETTh1's daily cyclic load is recorded descriptively. The per-variable axis remains aggregated under methodology § 2.10.1 and is blocked by the disaggregation doctrine; a per-variable evaluation script would lift this residual limit and additionally allow per-variable SHAP and VSN importance values to be cross-tabulated against per-variable error.

*Richer faithfulness metrics and stability-layer reinstatement.* SHAP, faithfulness, rank-agreement and trade-off scripts are all VALIDATED (§ 4.2); the remaining explainability work is methodological refinement. Three refinements would tighten RQ2 and RQ3: (i) a richer faithfulness metric (continuous infidelity, ordered-masking damage curves, or AUC-under-masking-curve) replacing the coarse top-vs-bottom rule of `faithfulness_test.py:495` to allow a criterion-robust re-test of the § 4.3 Pareto-optimality observation; (ii) a fair-core replacement for the dropped input-perturbation stability layer (prior `stability_test.py` used a protocol inconsistent with the locked implementation defaults, archived S-11 2026-04-20) to lift stability evidence above the seed-CV signal; (iii) per-variable SHAP and VSN importance export complementing the per-variable disaggregation item above.

*Multi-dataset extension.* All results are on ETTh1. Replicating the same fair-core protocol on at least one other multivariate forecasting dataset would directly address the external-validity caveat of § 4.4.3.

*Hyperparameter search under matched tuning budget.* A modest, budget-matched hyperparameter search (the same number of trials per model) would test whether the TFT-WORST observation persists under a richer tuning budget. This would not violate the comparable-tuning-budget rule provided the budget is matched across models.

*Independence-aware uncertainty.* The paired **moving-block** bootstrap at block length L = 96 method-matches the overlapping-sliding-window structure; the residual dependence risk is acknowledged in the uncertainty discussion rather than silently ignored. Block-length sensitivity across L ∈ {24, 48, 96, 192} (Appendix § F.2) confirms the intervals are robust to reasonable L perturbations. A stationary-bootstrap variant with geometrically-distributed block lengths would further relax the fixed-block assumption; this is flagged as optional future work but not required for the present uncertainty claim.
