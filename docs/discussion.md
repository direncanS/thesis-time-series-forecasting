## 4.1 Interpretation of Predictive Results (RQ1)

The observed overall ordering on every reported metric, under the present configuration, is **LR < MLP < LSTM < TFT** (lower error first; § 3.8.1 point estimates, § 3.8.2 paired-bootstrap intervals). All six pairwise MSE intervals exclude zero. RQ1 — *under identical task and information constraints, do more complex models outperform simpler baselines?* — is therefore answered in the negative under the present configuration: each step up the architectural-complexity ladder is associated with a higher observed error. *Interval estimates are used as comparative uncertainty evidence, not as a claim of strict independent-sample inference.*

Two counterintuitive observations are part of this finding: LR outperforms MLP (mean MSE diff −1.30, 95 % CI [−1.39, −1.22]; SUSPECT-02) and MLP outperforms LSTM (−2.95, 95 % CI [−3.16, −2.76]; SUSPECT-03). Both cleared the Counterintuitive-Result Trigger of § 11B (5 of 7 items PASS; residual two deferred per § 11C and § 2.10.3). The LR-leads-MLP observation has independent literature support in recent work showing that simple linear baselines can match or outperform transformer-class architectures on long-sequence time-series benchmarks including the ETT family (Zeng et al., 2023). The LSTM-above-MLP ordering is harder to ground in canonical literature — the closest framing is the capacity / configuration trade-off analysis of Ouyang, Ravier and Jabloun (2022) — and admits a partial mechanical reading anchored to methodology § 2.10.6: under the locked § 10 hidden width of 64, every forecast signal must pass through a single 64-dimensional hidden state before the 168-dimensional output head, whereas the MLP connects its 672-dimensional input directly to the 168-dimensional output through two 128-dimensional hidden layers with no analogous compression stage. Part of the observed gap is therefore consistent, under the present configuration, with a capacity-for-this-task constraint imposed by the 64-dimensional bottleneck rather than with a model-class inferiority claim. Both framings are preliminary and require further validation under the caveats of § 4.4.

The TFT is the worst of the four core models on overall MSE (TFT-ORDER-01); the ordering is robust under the loss-symmetric training cycle of S-02b. The interpretation is bounded on two axes. First, this thesis used the locked default `pytorch-forecasting` hyperparameters for TFT without a hyperparameter search, in line with the comparable-tuning-budget clause of § 2.5. Second, the TFT is also the smallest of the four models by parameter count (TFT 18 261 < LSTM 29 608 < LR 113 064 < MLP 124 328; `results/complexity_metrics.csv`); per § 2.10.6, part of the TFT-worst ordering is consistent with a capacity-constraint observation anchored to the `hidden_size = 16` default rather than with a transformer-family-inferiority claim. Under a richer tuning budget or a parameter-budget-matched variant the ordering could differ (§ 4.4.3).

Two framing points close this section. The **ensemble effect** (ENSEMBLE-EFFECT-01): seed-averaged ensemble MSE drops by 0.40 (MLP), 0.75 (LSTM), and 1.39 (TFT) relative to per-seed metric means, proportional to per-seed prediction variance (Jensen's inequality for squared-error loss); the ranking is preserved under both reporting choices. The **three-way orthogonality of complexity** (§ 3.8.3): parameter-count (MLP > LR > LSTM > TFT), architectural-category (LR < MLP < LSTM < TFT), and accuracy (LR best, TFT worst) rank the four models along different axes. RQ1 is most cleanly answered when complexity is read as architectural category — along that axis the present configuration produces a monotone anti-correlation with accuracy; along the parameter-count axis no such monotone relationship holds.

## 4.2 Interpretation of Explainability Results (RQ2)

The aggregated SHAP attribution profiles for LR, MLP, and LSTM and the VSN importance vector for TFT are reported in § 3.8.4. Per the interpretability contract (§ 13), interpretability is operationalised at the level of aggregated, model-level feature-importance comparison; faithfulness is **tested**, not assumed. The SHAP scripts (`src/explainability/shap_{lr,mlp,lstm}.py`), the faithfulness-test script (`src/explainability/faithfulness_test.py`), the cross-model rank-agreement script (`src/explainability/cross_model_xai_agreement.py`), and the trade-off plot in its post-S-15.5 strength-based form (`src/explainability/trade_off_plot.py`) are all VALIDATED in `docs/VALIDATION_LOG.md` (signed `ml-experiment-auditor`, 2026-04-21). A separate explanation-stability layer was dropped in the S-11 archive migration on 2026-04-20 (the prior `stability_test.py` used a non-§ 10-compliant training protocol and was superseded rather than refactored); stability evidence in this thesis is therefore restricted to the seed-CV signal already present in the 3-seed training pipeline. Under § 20 the explanation results are described as **preliminary but directionally supportive** in the sense calibrated by the S-14 / S-15 senior-ML review: the underlying scripts are code-level-correct, but the decision rules behind the numbers are weaker than a first reading suggests, and the prose of this chapter therefore carries interpretation constraints rather than conclusive strength claims (§ 11C *under the present configuration*).

The SHAP attributions for LR / MLP / LSTM and the VSN importance for TFT are *not* methodologically equivalent objects. SHAP is a post-hoc attribution layer applied to a fixed prediction function, whereas VSN is an architecture-native component whose weights are co-optimised with the rest of the network. Direct numeric comparison between a SHAP value and a VSN weight is therefore not meaningful; only the *rank ordering* of features within each model can be compared across the two methods, and even that comparison must acknowledge the post-hoc / architecture-native gap as a construct-validity limit (§ 4.4.2 below). Empirically the distinction has a signature on the S-15 rank-agreement analysis (`results/xai_agreement.csv`): the three SHAP-family pairs converge strongly on a common feature-importance rank (LR↔MLP Spearman 0.786, LR↔LSTM 0.857, MLP↔LSTM 0.714), whereas the three SHAP↔VSN pairs involving the TFT all fall below 0.5 (LR↔TFT 0.357, MLP↔TFT 0.107, LSTM↔TFT 0.286). This is reported as an empirical anchor for the method-family distinction required by § 13, not as a statement about TFT-native explanation quality relative to post-hoc SHAP. Position-count diagnostics — for instance the within-pair 1 / 7 and 0 / 7 feature-position matches recorded in the S-14 audit log — are retained as a supplementary artefact only; they materially understate the Spearman-level agreement and are not treated as the primary cross-model evidence.

The faithfulness layer is also calibrated along dimensions surfaced by the S-14 senior-ML review. The `src/explainability/faithfulness_test.py:495` decision rule is `top_k_increase > bottom_k_increase` without a minimum separation threshold; the run produces a 12 / 12 binary-faithful verdict across all four models, which is a **decision-rule count and not a strength measure**. Marginal cases are visible on the per-(model, k) gap: the LR k = 3 case is near-neutral (top ≈ +168.1 %, bottom ≈ +167.0 %), and the LSTM k = 1 bottom-mask effect is slightly negative (−4.2 %, i.e. removing the least-important variable marginally improves the LSTM). The gap-mean summary used by the RQ3 trade-off analysis of § 4.3 re-expresses this as a continuous strength axis under a criterion-bound reading. RQ2 — *how stable and faithful are model explanations across the compared models?* — is therefore answered, under the present configuration, in the directionally-supportive sense: within-SHAP-family rank agreement is strong on ETTh1; the method-family SHAP / VSN distinction is empirically visible; the adopted faithfulness criterion passes for all four core models, but its discrimination strength is limited by the coarse top-vs-bottom masking rule. Per-seed SHAP magnitudes for the LSTM show moderate variance (e.g. HUFL mean ≈ 0.0054, std ≈ 0.0019, coefficient of variation ≈ 35 %); the top-ranked feature set is preserved across seeds, while per-seed importance magnitudes show moderate variance. Richer faithfulness metrics are flagged as future work (§ 4.6).

## 4.3 Trade-off Analysis (RQ3)

RQ3 — *under the same experimental conditions, is there evidence of a trade-off between predictive performance and interpretability?* — is answered, under the present configuration, by a two-axis inspection of the four core models on (overall MSE, faithfulness-gap) coordinates. The accuracy axis is the seed-averaged original-scale MSE of § 3.8.2 (LR 7.66 / MLP 9.36 / LSTM 12.71 / TFT 14.19). The interpretability axis is the **faithfulness-gap mean** produced by `src/explainability/trade_off_plot.py` after the S-15.5 patch of 2026-04-21, defined as the across-k mean of `top_k_mse_increase − bottom_k_mse_increase` over k ∈ {1, 2, 3}. The gap_mean is derived from the same upstream `results/faithfulness.csv` as the binary 12 / 12 pass-rate summary it supersedes; no `faithfulness_test.py` rerun was performed, and the derivation is documented in the module docstring and in `docs/VALIDATION_LOG.md` under `src/explainability/trade_off_plot.py (post-S-15.5 strength-based metric)`.

Under the adopted faithfulness-gap metric the linear baseline occupies the strongest point on both axes. Its overall MSE of 7.66 is the lowest of the four core models, and its gap_mean of 0.812 is the highest against MLP 0.173, LSTM 0.341, and TFT 0.234 (values manual-verified to within ±0.001 of `results/trade_off_data.csv`). Under the present configuration the LR therefore occupies a **Pareto-optimal position under the adopted faithfulness-gap metric**, and no strong-form trade-off between predictive accuracy and this operationalisation of interpretability is observed under the present setup.

The result is, however, **criterion-dependent**. LR's gap_mean is driven primarily by the k = 1 contribution (gap ≈ 2.21); the separation collapses at k = 3 (gap ≈ 0.004) and the across-k dispersion is large (gap_std ≈ 1.22; see `results/faithfulness_gap_by_k.png` for the per-model k-decomposition). The underlying decision rule in `src/explainability/faithfulness_test.py:495` is a coarse top-vs-bottom masking comparison without a minimum separation threshold, and — as the S-14 / S-15 senior-review prose calibration records — the rule originally produced a 12 / 12 binary-faithful verdict for every (model, k) cell in the run, so the gap_mean is best read as a strength-differentiator applied to an already-weakly-discriminating underlying rule. Two observational non-monotonicities in the gap_mean vector reinforce the criterion-dependence framing under § 11C's "under the present configuration" discipline: the MLP is the second-most-accurate model but has the **lowest** gap_mean of the four (0.173, below the TFT's 0.234), and the TFT has the highest gap_min of 0.213 (the most k-stable faithfulness profile) despite having the worst overall MSE. Neither observation is interpreted causally in this thesis; both are descriptive.

A richer faithfulness metric — continuous infidelity measures, ordered-masking damage curves, or AUC-under-masking-curve diagnostics — would be required to test RQ3's strong form under a criterion less susceptible to k = 1 dominance; such a metric is identified as future work in § 4.6. The S-15 cross-model rank-agreement analysis (`results/xai_agreement.csv`) reinforces a method-family caveat that any such richer probe must address as well: the three SHAP-family pairs converge strongly on a common feature-importance rank (LR↔MLP Spearman 0.786, LR↔LSTM 0.857, MLP↔LSTM 0.714), whereas the three SHAP↔VSN pairs involving the TFT land uniformly below 0.5 (LR↔TFT 0.357, MLP↔TFT 0.107, LSTM↔TFT 0.286), empirically anchoring the post-hoc vs architecture-native distinction of § 13 and § 4.2. RQ3 in the present thesis is therefore answered only under the adopted gap_mean criterion and only under the SHAP / VSN construct-validity limit recorded in § 4.4.2; a full-strength answer is outside the scope of this bachelor thesis.

## 4.4 Threats to Validity

This section enumerates the threats to validity in the three named subsections required by § 17.

### 4.4.1 Internal validity (§ 17 Internal validity ↔ Discussion § 4.4.1)

Seven internal-validity items are recorded.

*TFT seed-42 best-epoch (SUSPECT-01).* Best epoch 5 is borderline to the {1, 2, 3, 4} early-convergence trigger of § 17, but the validation-loss trajectory descends 41 / 51 / 54 % from epoch 0 to best epoch across seeds 42 / 123 / 456 — the trajectory is not flat, no early-stopping pathology evidence, recorded for transparency only.

*TFT seed-variance gain (resolved).* Diagnostic-cycle TFT had relative per-seed MSE std 23.5 % (TFT-SEED-VAR-01); the thesis-valid cycle (post loss-symmetry fix S-02b aligning TFT with the MLP / LSTM MSE objective) has 4.8 %, a fivefold reduction attributed to the fix. Prior diagnostic-cycle numbers are not thesis evidence.

*Ensemble-vs-single distinction.* ENSEMBLE-EFFECT-01 model-dependent reductions are documented in § 4.1; the thesis reports both conventions (per-seed mean in § 3.8.1, ensemble in § 3.8.2) and the ordering holds under both.

*Sliding-window independence.* ETTh1 sliding windows overlap, violating the standard-bootstrap independence assumption. The § 16 safety sentence applies wherever intervals are quoted.

*Checkpoint and seed isolation.* The fail-fast guard at restore-best (§ 3.4) was added as a code-robustness pass; in the locked baseline runs it never fires.

*Clipping-symmetrisation audit (CLIP-SYMMETRY-01).* The diagnostic-cycle training protocols for MLP, LSTM and TFT differed in one inherited regulariser: the `pytorch-forecasting` TFT trainer applies `gradient_clip_val = 0.1` by default, while the MLP and LSTM trainers in `src/training/multi_seed.py` applied no clipping. S-12 (2026-04-21) symmetrised this via a single `clip_grad_norm_(max_norm=0.1)` call in the shared `train_with_early_stopping` helper at `src/training/multi_seed.py:133` (LR is closed-form OLS; § 11A item 8 deterministic-baseline exception). `ml-experiment-auditor` re-audited and recorded PASS (`docs/VALIDATION_LOG.md`, 2026-04-21). Methodology § 2.10.5 documents the full pre / post delta table: seed-mean shifts are ≈ 0 % (LR, MLP, TFT) and +0.3 % (LSTM); the overall ordering is preserved. A symmetry correction rather than a hyperparameter tune.

*Device and precision asymmetry (CPU FP32 vs GPU TF32).* MLP and LSTM train on CPU FP32; TFT on GPU under `torch.set_float32_matmul_precision("high")` (PyTorch Lightning default for Ampere-class and newer GPUs, standard in the ETTh1 transformer-family benchmarks of Zhou et al., 2021; Zeng et al., 2023). Methodology § 2.10.6 records the observed impact as sub-fourth-decimal; carried here as an internal-validity caveat.

### 4.4.2 Construct validity (§ 17 Construct validity ↔ Discussion § 4.4.2)

Four construct-validity items are recorded.

*Interpretability is operationalised narrowly.* Per § 13, interpretability here means **aggregated, model-level feature-importance comparison**, not a claim of causal understanding or a mechanistic account of why specific features matter. The narrow construct is further constrained by the SHAP / VSN asymmetry (§ 4.2): SHAP is post-hoc, VSN architecture-native, not equivalent measurement instruments. The S-15 rank-agreement analysis (`results/xai_agreement.csv`) provides the empirical anchor: SHAP-family pairs converge strongly (LR↔MLP Spearman 0.786, LR↔LSTM 0.857, MLP↔LSTM 0.714), SHAP↔VSN pairs involving the TFT all fall below 0.5 (LR↔TFT 0.357, MLP↔TFT 0.107, LSTM↔TFT 0.286). Under § 11C the observation is descriptive and does not support a stronger claim about TFT-native explanation quality.

*Per-variable visibility constraint.* The § 2.10.1 aggregation contract reports a single scalar mean over the (windows × 24 × 7) tensor; per-variable claims are blocked by the § 11C disaggregation doctrine. A per-variable evaluation path is future work (§ 4.6).

*Loss-magnitude asymmetry.* The `nn.MSELoss()` reduction in the MLP / LSTM training path collapses to a single scalar mean over `(batch × horizon × variable)`; the `MultiLoss([MSE() for 7])` construction in the TFT path sums seven per-feature MSE terms (≈ 7× magnitude). Gradient direction is identical; Adam's second-moment normalisation together with the TFT learning rate `1 × 10^{-3}` absorbs the magnitude difference. The § 11A item 8 "training-loss symmetry" is therefore interpreted as **gradient-direction symmetry, not loss-magnitude symmetry**, per methodology § 2.10.6. Retraining under unified scalar-mean MSE is outside bachelor scope.

*Criterion-dependence of the faithfulness-gap metric.* The RQ3 trade-off analysis of § 4.3 uses the faithfulness-gap mean across k ∈ {1, 2, 3} as the interpretability axis. The underlying decision rule in `src/explainability/faithfulness_test.py:495` is a coarse top-vs-bottom masking comparison without a minimum separation threshold, and the across-k mean aggregation is susceptible to k = 1 dominance — as exhibited by the LR gap_mean, whose k = 1 contribution of ≈ 2.21 dominates a k = 3 contribution of ≈ 0.004 (`results/faithfulness_gap_by_k.png`). The "LR is Pareto-optimal" observation of § 4.3 is therefore a **criterion-bound** claim: it holds under the adopted gap_mean, would need to be re-examined under a continuous infidelity metric, an ordered-masking damage curve, or an AUC-under-masking-curve diagnostic, and does not support a stronger-form claim about LR-superior explanation quality in general. Richer faithfulness metrics are flagged as future work in § 4.6.

### 4.4.3 External validity (§ 17 External validity ↔ Discussion § 4.4.3)

Four external-validity items are recorded.

*Single dataset.* All results are on ETTh1 (Zhou et al., 2021); no general-domain generalisation is claimed. A multi-dataset extension is future work.

*Locked default tuning budget for TFT.* The TFT was run with the locked default `pytorch-forecasting` hyperparameters (hidden size 16, 4 heads, dropout 0.1, lr 1e-3) without a hyperparameter search, consistent with the comparable-tuning-budget clause of § 11 (no model received an asymmetric budget). The TFT-worst observation of § 4.1 is therefore bounded by the budget under which it was obtained; a richer search could change the ordering.

*Equalised past-only information regime.* `time_varying_known_reals=[]` is required by § 2.5 so all four models receive identical information at prediction time. This removes the TFT's native support for known-future covariates (e.g. calendar variables), which the other three architectures cannot consume anyway. The TFT-worst observation of § 4.1 should be read as *given the same information as a linear baseline on this dataset, the TFT does not produce a lower error*; a re-deployment with known-future calendar covariates would lift this constraint and could yield a different ordering. That alternative thesis is methodologically distinct and not pursued here.

*Non-canonical chronological split (60 / 20 / 20 row-based).* This thesis partitions ETTh1 at 60 / 20 / 20 on a row basis (CLAUDE.md § 4); parts of the ETTh1 transformer-family literature use a 12 / 4 / 4-month calendar-based partition (Zhou et al., 2021; Zeng et al., 2023). Effective forecast span + split-then-window protocol recorded in methodology § 2.3.1 (METHOD-NOTE-01); § 2.10.6 carries the divergence as an acknowledged design asymmetry. External benchmark comparisons in this chapter must point back to METHOD-NOTE-01 rather than treat numbers as apples-to-apples. Recorded here as an external-validity caveat rather than a fairness-protocol violation, since all four core models share identical partition boundaries.

## 4.5 Generalisability

The framework itself is the reusable contribution. The dataset-agnostic components — split-then-window preprocessing, best-checkpoint training, frozen-tensor prediction export, four-axis original-scale evaluation, paired-bootstrap uncertainty, and the parameter-count / arch / wall-clock complexity layer — would carry over to any hourly multivariate forecasting task with a similar horizon structure. The Fairness and Comparability Protocol (§ 2.5) and the Comparability Gate (§ 11A) are independent of the specific models and would constrain any fair-core extension to other architectures. Task-specific parts are the four model implementations and the SHAP / VSN explainability branch, both of which carry pytorch-forecasting and SHAP-library-specific assumptions.

## 4.6 Future Work

Five concrete future-work items are identified.

*Per-variable disaggregation export.* A per-horizon path is already in place (`src/evaluation/per_horizon_metrics.py`, VALIDATED 2026-04-21) and § 11C admits descriptive per-horizon observations — the non-monotonic LSTM h = 24 < h = 12 and TFT h = 24 < h = 12 pattern consistent with ETTh1's daily cyclic load is recorded descriptively. The per-variable axis remains aggregated under methodology § 2.10.1 and is blocked by the § 11C disaggregation doctrine; a per-variable evaluation script would lift this residual limit and additionally allow per-variable SHAP and VSN importance values to be cross-tabulated against per-variable error.

*Richer faithfulness metrics and stability-layer reinstatement.* SHAP, faithfulness, rank-agreement and trade-off scripts are all VALIDATED (§ 4.2); the remaining explainability work is methodological refinement. Three refinements would tighten RQ2 and RQ3: (i) a richer faithfulness metric (continuous infidelity, ordered-masking damage curves, or AUC-under-masking-curve) replacing the coarse top-vs-bottom rule of `faithfulness_test.py:495` to allow a criterion-robust re-test of the § 4.3 Pareto-optimality observation; (ii) a fair-core replacement for the dropped input-perturbation stability layer (prior `stability_test.py` used a non-§ 10 protocol, archived S-11 2026-04-20) to lift stability evidence above the seed-CV signal; (iii) per-variable SHAP and VSN importance export complementing the per-variable disaggregation item above.

*Multi-dataset extension.* All results are on ETTh1. Replicating the same fair-core protocol on at least one other multivariate forecasting dataset would directly address the external-validity caveat of § 4.4.3.

*Hyperparameter search under matched tuning budget.* A modest, budget-matched hyperparameter search (the same number of trials per model) would test whether the TFT-WORST observation persists under a richer tuning budget. This would not violate the comparable-tuning-budget rule provided the budget is matched across models.

*Independence-aware uncertainty.* The paired bootstrap on overlapping sliding windows carries a residual dependence risk (§ 4.4.1). A block-bootstrap or stationary-bootstrap variant that respects the temporal-overlap structure would tighten the inferential claim associated with the intervals.
